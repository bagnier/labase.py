Something grated and the user stopped to name it. They are not asking for a repair — they
are asking what was in your head. Answer that, briefly, and the work resumes exactly where
it stood.

The value of this is that it happens *now*. In ten turns the belief is gone: overwritten
by the correction, rationalised into something more flattering, or compacted away. Right
now it is still there to be read off.

## What you owe

Short prose — a handful of lines, not a form. These are the doors worth opening; open the
ones that have something behind them:

- **the belief, stated flat.** "I read `--to` as naming the projects to write to, so a
  project that never took the charm looked like an error to report." Not "I may have
  misinterpreted the scope" — that says nothing.
- **where it came from**, named precisely: the sentence in a skill, the example that
  looked canonical, the convention you carried in from elsewhere, the ambiguity you
  resolved silently. If nothing said it and you guessed, say you guessed.
- **what you never saw** — the file you did not open, the line further down that
  contradicted you, the part of the request you dropped. And what would have changed the
  decision.
- **whether the reproach lands.** It may not. If it was deliberate, say so and say why —
  "no, I did that on purpose, because the alternative would have X". Folding at the first
  frown wastes the exchange: the user is trying to find out how the thing thinks, and an
  answer that just agrees teaches them nothing.

## Do not confabulate

This is the whole difficulty. Asked why you did something, a plausible causal story
assembles itself effortlessly, and it can be entirely invented after the fact — you are
narrating a decision, not replaying it. A tidy explanation is exactly what a confabulation
looks like.

So: only claim a cause you can point at. If you cannot reconstruct it, **"I don't know why
I did that" is the correct answer** and a genuinely useful one — it says the behaviour came
from nowhere nameable, which is itself the finding. Prefer it to a well-turned story.

Where you are unsure, mark it: "I think it came from X, but I may be reconstructing that."

## Do not repair

- No fix, no edit, no rerun. The user will say when to resume.
- Do not rewrite the skill or instruction in question — noticing it is undermined is not
  authority to change it, and the fix belongs to a decision of its own.
- No apology, no promise to do better, no paragraph of contrition. It is not a fault, it
  is a reading.
- Do not restate the task, re-plan, or slide back into the work. Answer, stop, wait.

## Close

One last line, so this is findable later in the record:

```
🪤 gotcha — <the belief, in a dozen words>
```
